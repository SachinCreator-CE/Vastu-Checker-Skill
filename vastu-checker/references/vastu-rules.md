# Vastu rules

Distilled from a compilation on Vastu Shastra that draws on the Mayamata, the Brihat Samhita, the Vasturaja Vallabha and the Tamil Manaiyadi Sastram, and restated here in original words; the compilation itself is not included. Where the compilation contradicts itself, both readings are kept and marked **contested**. Directions are true compass directions (N, NE, E, SE, S, SW, W, NW), with C for the centre (Brahmasthan).

## 1. How the source frames Vastu

- The rules combine practical reasons (morning sun from the east, rain draining to the low side, protection from harsh afternoon sun) with the deities of the Vastu Purusha Mandala who rule each part of the site.
- They must be read for modern conditions. Old texts kept fire and water apart because a wet hearth wouldn't burn; a modern kitchen with a sink and a gas stove in one room is fine. Toilets were once kept at the far end of the property for hygiene; attached toilets are fine now.
- Few urban homes, flats especially, can follow every rule; the aim is the best adjustment available.
- No facing is bad in itself: a west-facing house is not a defect.
- Rituals can offset what cannot be corrected (section 11).

For the checker this means: report compliance honestly, rank by importance, prefer design fixes, and treat what can't be fixed as a trade-off for the family, not a disaster.

## 2. The grid

The Vastu Purusha is pictured lying face down across the site, head to the NE and feet to the SW. The full mandala is a 9×9 grid of 81 squares, each ruled by a deity, with Brahma in the central 3×3, which traditionally stays open. For planning, the source groups the squares into a 5×5 grid for larger homes and a 3×3 grid for small ones.

**3×3 allocation for a small house** (north at the top):

| NW: guest room | N: cash and valuables | NE: puja |
|---|---|---|
| **W: dining** | **C: open space** | **E: bathing room** |
| **SW: store, heavy items** | **S: bedroom** | **SE: kitchen** |

**5×5 positions** (house column): NE corner puja; N side, from east to west, drawing room, cash or treasury (Kubera's square at the N centre), family room; NW corner guest room. E side, from north to south, drawing room, bathing room, store; SE corner kitchen. S side, from east to west, store, junior bedroom, toilet; SW corner master bedroom. W side, from south to north, senior bedroom, dining, junior bedroom.

**Direction, ruling planet and suitable rooms** (the source's astrology table):

| Direction | Planet | Rooms |
|---|---|---|
| N | Mercury | living room, safe |
| NE | Jupiter | puja, living room |
| E | Sun | living room, bathing room without WC |
| SE | Venus | kitchen |
| S | Mars | kitchen, store |
| SW | Rahu | master bedroom, heavy storage |
| W | Saturn | children's bedroom, store, study |
| NW | Moon | guest room, bathroom, grain store |

The pattern behind all of it: keep the NE light, low, open and clean (water, worship, openings); make the SW heavy, high and closed (master bedroom, heavy storage, stairs, overhead tank); fire in the SE; air, movement and guests in the NW; the centre open.

## 3. The plot

**Ground**
- Flat and level is best. A fall toward N or E is acceptable; toward W or S is not.
- Avoid land on a hill, in a valley, beside pits or depressions, or humped or sunk at the centre.
- The NE half (the "solar" half) should sit lower and the SW half (the "lunar" half) higher, so water runs from W to E and from S to N. The NE corner should be the lowest point. Ranked by which side is lowest: NE, E and N are good; NW is mixed; W, SW, S and SE are bad.

**Shape and alignment**
- Square is best, then rectangular, with sides facing the four cardinal directions. Up to about 20° of tilt is acceptable. If the plot isn't aligned, set the house itself square to the cardinal directions.
- In a rectangle, longer sides running N–S are better than E–W.
- Octagonal plots are auspicious; regular hexagonal plots are acceptable when everything else complies.
- Avoid round, oval, triangular, L-shaped and irregular plots, and plots with necks, bulges or cross shapes (the source's cart, drum, damru, pot, bow and similar shapes). Regularise them with a wall where possible.
- A plot narrower at the front and wider at the back (gaumukh, "cow-faced") suits homes, especially with roads on S and W. Wider at the front (simha-mukh, "lion-faced") suits business, not homes.
- If the plot isn't a true rectangle, at least the SW and SE sides should meet at a right angle, and the SW side should never run out beyond the SE side.

**Extensions and cut corners**
- An extension at the NE (toward N or E) is good.
- Extensions elsewhere are defects: NW extended toward N, SE toward E or S, SW toward S or W. (NW extended toward W is listed as suiting public-facing work; not a home recommendation.)
- A missing or cut NE corner is the most serious plot defect; the source advises against buying. Cut NW, SE or SW corners are also defects. Fix by buying the missing piece, or square off the usable plot with a wall.
- Separate structures in the plot's corners: NE is the worst, SE and NW are bad, SW is allowed if it doesn't block a path.

**Roads**
- Roads on all four sides: best. On E and N: good. On N and W: average. On N, W and E, or on N, S and W: not so good. On E, S and W: bad.
- A road along the E or N side is better than one along the S or W.
- A road running along the plot and ending at its NE corner is very good; ending at any other corner usually isn't.
- T and Y junctions are inauspicious unless the road meets a favourable spot (next item).
- **Road hits** (veedhi shoola, a road aimed straight at the plot): favourable when it hits the north part of the NE, the east part of the NE, the south part of the SE or the west part of the NW. Unfavourable at the east part of the SE and the north part of the NW. Seriously unfavourable at the south or west part of the SW.

**Facing** (the source's associations, none forbidden): E for scholars, teachers and priests; N for administration and government work; S for business; W for service work.

**Surroundings**
- Tall buildings, towers or hills on the N, NE or E block the morning sun: avoid. On the S or W they block the afternoon sun: fine.
- Water (river, lake, pool, well) to the NE: good. To the SW: bad.
- Avoid an electrical substation or a large pole at the NE, a bridge on the N or E side, a plot squeezed between two larger plots, former burial or cremation grounds, and neighbours such as meat shops, workshops and laundries.
- Keep the house more than twice its height from a temple, hospital or factory; the source adds at least 80 ft from a temple, with the gate not facing it and its shadow not falling on the house.

**Soil**
- Prefer soil that can grow plants. Avoid rocky ground, ground full of worms, bones or debris, and black clayey soil. Expansive black clay is also a foundation problem, so hand it to the structural step.
- The source's traditional pit tests: dig a 2 ft cube and refill it (soil left over is good, an exact fill average, a shortfall poor), then fill it with water (absorption taking more than an hour is good; heavy cracking after it drains calls for stronger foundations). Treat these as traditional indicators, not a substitute for a soil investigation.

## 4. Site layout

**House position and open space**
- Place the house toward the SW of the plot, leaving more open space on N and E than on S and W. A large open area on the W is unfavourable.

**Compound wall and gates**
- Build the compound wall before the house, starting from the SW; keep it lower than the house.
- Make it higher and heavier on S and W. The N and E walls should be lower, by about 53 cm (21 in) per the source, or at least 8 cm (3 in).
- Gates on N, NE or E are best; W is acceptable; avoid S. On a south-road plot, put the gate toward the SE end, not the SW; on a west-road plot, toward the NW end, not the SW.
- Two gates are better than one. The property gate and the house's main door should be on the same side.

**Vedha (obstructions facing the gate or main door)**
- A big tree, a ditch or open drain, an open well, the corner of another building, a straight street aimed at the door, a ruined building, staircase railings, pillars, or electric and telephone poles.
- They don't count when a public road runs between, or when they stand more than twice the house's height away (for an 11 ft house, beyond 22 ft).

**Water**
- Well, borewell, underground sump, or the municipal supply's entry point: NE best, then N or E. Never in the SW, SE or NW corners, and not in the middle of the house.
- Keep the well to one side of the corner diagonal rather than on it. The compilation's wording names a line from the SE to the NE corner; most texts use the NE–SW diagonal.
- Wells should be round, get several hours of sun, and not be shared with a neighbour.
- Kitchen and bathroom waste and rainwater should flow toward the NE and leave the plot from that side; never out through the SW or SE.

**Septic tank**: between N and NW, or between E and SE (from the source's office and factory section, commonly applied to homes). Never under the main entrance; keep it well away from the borewell or sump, following local health norms.

**Overhead water tank**: SW best, W acceptable. NW only if small and set about 1 m in from the corner. Never NE, SE or over the middle of the roof. Raise it at least 1 m above the roof on a platform; one passage prefers that it not touch the main building. Use separate tanks for kitchen and bathrooms where possible. A plastic tank should be dark (black or dark blue).

**Gardens and trees**
- Tulsi on the N, NE or E, or in front of the house.
- Lawns, fountains, pools and low plants (under about 1 m) on the N, E or NE.
- Tall trees on the S and W, never on the N, NE or E, and no tree at all in the NE corner. One passage also keeps gardens out of the SE and SW corners.
- No tree directly in front of the main entrance, no tree shadow on the house, roots far enough not to damage foundations or walls, and no branches touching the house. An even number of trees.
- Rock gardens and heavy sculpture in the SW.
- No thorny plants near the house (roses excepted) and no cactus. Creepers on their own supports, not on the house or compound wall. Banyan and peepal belong near temples.
- Trees the source favours: coconut, neem, betel, sandalwood, lemon, pineapple, bilva, almond, jackfruit, pomegranate, mango, amla, katha.
- Swings on the N or E, swinging E–W. Outdoor seating on the S or W side of an open area, facing N or E.

**Outbuildings** (garage, servant quarters, annexes): SE or NW, about 1 m clear of the boundary walls, separate from the house, never touching the N or E walls, and not on the N or E side of the house.

**Parking**: NW of the plot is best; avoid SW and NE. Park cars facing E or N, never S. Basement parking on the N or E side. A garage shouldn't attach to the compound wall or the house; paint it light.

**Generators, transformers, noisy machinery**: SE, never NE, and away from the house.

**Swimming pool**: NE, set into the zone rather than exactly on the corner.

**Guard room**: near the gate with a clear view, facing E or N, never NE. **Contested**: one passage puts the guard in the SE and not the NW or SW; another allows the SW.

## 5. The building

- **Proportion**: length to breadth 1:1, 1:1.5, never beyond 1:2. Longer side N–S rather than E–W.
- **Height and weight**: either uniform, or higher on S, SW and W and lower on N, NE and E; never higher at the NE than the SW. S and W walls thicker where possible. If only part of an upper floor is built, build it over the SW.
- **Levels**: ground and floor levels higher on S, SW, SE and W and lower on N, NE and E, with the centre even. Water inside flows to E or N.
- **Roof**: slope down toward the NE, or equally on all sides. With a one-way slope keep the S and W high and bring the E or N side down; never slope only toward the S or W. Rainwater to the N, E or NE.
- **Openings**: more doors and windows on N and E. Fewer openings on upper floors than on the ground floor, and not the same number. Doors plus windows on each floor should total an even number that doesn't end in zero (not 10, 20, 30). Keep good cross-ventilation.
- **Projections**: uniform; terraces, porticos, porches and balconies only on N, E or NE.
- **Centre (Brahmasthan)**: keep it open, light and uncluttered: no toilet, well, heavy storage, heavy machinery or water tank over it. A courtyard or open-to-sky space there is the traditional ideal.
- **Ceilings**: upper-floor rooms lower than ground-floor rooms.
- **Columns and beams**: an even number (minor).
- **Mezzanine**: W or S.

## 7. Main entrance and doors

**Main door position**
- Best on the N or E wall; W acceptable; avoid S.
- Not at the exact centre of a wall and not at the extreme corners. Shift it: on the N wall, east of centre; on the E wall, north of centre; on the S wall, east of centre; on the W wall, south of centre (some authorities centre a west door). Another method divides the wall into nine parts and uses the fourth.
- Schools differ on exact door positions (pada systems). If the family follows a consultant's padas, use theirs.

**Main door form**
- The largest door in the house, two shutters opening inward, teak preferred, with a threshold. Rectangular: not square, sliding, circular or slanted. Width about half the height.
- Not below ground level; no shadow falling on it; no blank wall immediately inside (a door into the next room instead).
- Not directly opposite a neighbour's main door; no entrance shared with another house; no tank, septic tank or drain underneath; no ruins, rubbish, dirty water or water body in front.
- If the entrance is on the S, no balcony or veranda in front of it.
- Two entrances are good: the second smaller, single-shuttered, and not in a straight line with the first.
- Shoes to one side, not in front of the door.

**Other doors**
- Doors shouldn't line up so that one opens straight into another.
- Doors open into rooms. Bedroom doors on E, W or N walls, single shutter.

## 8. Stairs, basement, upper floors, balconies

- **Step count**: odd, and never ending in zero. One passage lists 9, 11 and 15; another wants a count leaving remainder 2 when divided by 3 (11, 17, 23). Counts that satisfy both: 5, 11, 17, 23, 29.
- **Climb**: facing W or S up to the midpoint is auspicious, and arriving at the NE of the upper floor is good. The source also says the top doorway should sit lower than the bottom one (about 20 cm); minor.
- **Upper floors**: bedrooms and studies upstairs, heavy storage downstairs; fewer openings upstairs; lower ceilings upstairs; balconies on N, E or NE, not SW.
- **Basement**: see the zone table.

## 10. Living-in tips

Not plan matters, but families often ask:
- Sleep with the head to the S or E at home (W when away); never to the N. Place beds so you face N or E on getting up. Four-legged beds rather than box beds.
- In a bedroom: wardrobe on the NW or SW side; TV, heater and AC in the SE; don't leave the SW corner empty; no deity images; a small night light.
- Keep the NE corner of every room empty, or furniture about 15 cm off the wall there.
- Heavy furniture to the S and W of a room. The owner sits facing N or E, guests facing W or S.
- Study and business facing N, NE or E. Pray facing N or E.
- Mirrors on N or E walls, never S or W; no broken mirrors. Clocks on W, N or E walls. Wall cupboards on S or W walls.
- Washing in the SE; medicines in the NE.
- Avoid disturbing pictures (war, weeping, fighting animals, birds of prey).
- Colours the source suggests: bedroom light rose, blue, green or grey; living room white, yellow, green or blue (not red or black); bathroom white or sky blue; study white, sky blue, cream or light green; cash room yellow or mustard; puja white, light yellow or light blue; basement white.

## 11. Remedies the source mentions

For defects that can't be corrected by design: a Vastu shanti puja (part of the griha pravesh ceremony), a Vastu Ganesha at the puja place, and regular worship. The source's author holds that devotion outweighs Vastu defects. Timings for bhoomi puja, griha pravesh, digging a well or installing a safe are for a priest or astrologer; this skill doesn't set dates.

## 12. Left out, and why

The compilation also covers auspicious days and constellations for ceremonies, omens from objects found while digging, whom not to buy land from, and choosing soil colour by caste. These are either not design decisions or not appropriate to apply, so the checker leaves them out. The office and factory sections are left out too, except where noted above.
